Kamitani Lab investigates how the brain represents and processes information, developing techniques to decode and visualize mental contents from brain activity. Our research spans four interconnected areas.

## Brain Decoding

Decoding the contents of perception, imagery, and dreams from brain activity measured with fMRI. We develop machine learning methods to read out fine-grained neural representations in the human brain, including visual features, object categories, and subjective experiences.

**Key topics:**
- Visual image reconstruction from fMRI using deep neural network features
- Mental imagery and dream decoding
- Reconstruction of visual illusory experiences
- Neural code conversion across individuals

**Selected publications:**
- Kamitani & Tong (2005) *Nature Neuroscience* — Decoding the visual and subjective contents of the human brain
- Horikawa et al. (2013) *Science* — Neural decoding of visual imagery during sleep
- Shen et al. (2019) *PLOS Computational Biology* — Deep image reconstruction from human brain activity

---

## NeuroAI

Bridging neuroscience and artificial intelligence to understand shared computational principles between biological and artificial neural networks. We investigate how representations in deep neural networks relate to those in the brain, and how insights from one domain can advance the other.

**Key topics:**
- Comparison of hierarchical representations in visual cortex and DNNs
- Brain-to-image reconstruction using generative models (diffusion models, GANs)
- Understanding neural information processing through the lens of AI

---

## Brain-Machine Interface (BMI)

Developing technologies that translate brain signals into control commands for external devices. Our work encompasses both invasive (ECoG) and non-invasive (fMRI, EEG) approaches, aiming at assistive applications for patients with neurological conditions.

**Key topics:**
- Robot control from brain activity (ATR–Honda collaboration)
- High-density ECoG-based decoding of motor intentions
- Phantom limb-controlled BMI robot (Yanagisawa et al., *Nature Communications* 2016)
- Clinical applications for stroke and ALS patients
